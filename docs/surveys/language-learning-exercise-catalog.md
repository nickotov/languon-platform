# Language-learning exercise catalog and answer verification

## Recommendation

Build a catalog of named learning activities with reusable interaction formats and explicit checking policies. Start with vocabulary recall in context, grammar completion and transformation, source-grounded comprehension, and short productive tasks. Add matching/ordering and audio activities where their interaction or media dependencies are justified. Preserve user selection; let the agent choose a suitable mix only when selection is absent.

There is no defensible universal ranking of the exercises all language learners like most. Learning effectiveness, familiarity, enjoyment, and ease of implementation are different considerations. The catalog below is a product recommendation for Languon's confirmed audience of independent adult learners, not an experimentally established ordering. English examples illustrate mechanisms; actual generation must respect the selected language, script, dialect and level.

Research-only scope: names, descriptions, generation inputs/outputs, verification policies, selection rules and validation experiments. No new exercise types or runtime behavior are implemented or authorized by this survey. Audio and speaking are included for completeness, with separate prerequisites.

The [implementation plan](../named-exercises-implementation-plan.md) records the
subsequent delivery decisions: implement BL-014's isolated tutor/tool playground
first, let the user check it, then return to the deferred 19-activity text
catalog and later expansion. Only the playground feature is currently authorized
for implementation; the research findings themselves remain proposals.

## Evidence and interpretation

[Nation's Four Strands](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/2007-Four-strands.pdf) proposes balancing meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, deliberate language study and fluency development across a course. This is a curriculum framework, not proof that every five-question practice set needs equal quarters. It argues against making all practice isolated recognition quizzes.

A [meta-analysis of written corrective feedback](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/13621688221147374) aggregates 52 controlled studies and reports durable, moderate benefits for L2 accuracy. Transfer to arbitrary AI feedback is unproven. Our implication: offer specific feedback and a revision opportunity, then test new-item performance rather than only immediate correction.

[Research on form-focused instruction](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1362168818776671) synthesizes 54 studies involving 5,051 learners and reports beneficial overall effects. It supports targeted grammar work, not a universal preference for one UI format. Grammar practice should connect a form to its meaning and context, then require some production.

[Cambridge B2 First](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/first/format/) documents cloze, word formation, transformation, comprehension and productive tasks. [IELTS reading](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading) uses complementary tasks for gist, details and relationships in a text. These establish recognizable assessment patterns, not that practicing any test item causes broad proficiency gains. The catalog's concrete examples and priorities are our design proposals.

[Quizlet's study modes](https://quizlet.com/features/study-modes) demonstrate familiar flashcards, practice tests and matching. Its [grading controls](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048313652-Using-grading-options-US) also illustrate that accepted-answer strictness is a product choice. We should make strictness depend on the learning objective: tolerating an accent error may be reasonable for one comprehension task but invalidate a spelling or meaning distinction in another language.

Public product availability is only a proxy for familiarity, not exercise-level popularity or learner preference. Publisher abstracts and official pages were inspected; this is a targeted evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a user study of Languon.

Preference evidence is narrower than the overall learning literature:

- [Rosell-Aguilar's Busuu study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09588221.2018.1456465), summarized by the [Open University](https://research.open.ac.uk/news/can-you-learn-language-app-what-research-says), surveyed 4,095 self-selected users. Feedback was generally rated positively and opportunistic use was common. The historical, beginner-skewed sample supports testing brief, clear-feedback activities, not claiming a universal favorite format.
- [Hidaka and Sasao, 2024](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/letko/24/0/24_67/_article/-char/en) studied 107 Japanese university EFL learners over four weeks. Captioned video was most preferred/enjoyable; perceived usefulness did not differ significantly among reading, listening and captioned video. This supports an optional supported-input route, not presenting captions as proof of unaided listening.
- [Duolingo-affiliated AI-feature studies, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1499497/full) report positive perceptions of Roleplay and Explain My Answer and changes in some confidence measures. Self-report, attrition and absence of a control group limit causal interpretation. These are promising experience patterns, not proof of superior proficiency gains.
- [Duolingo's practice menu](https://blog.duolingo.com/guide-to-duolingo-practice-hub/), [Busuu Conversations](https://www.busuu.com/en/languages/language-learning-with-busuu-conversations), and [Speak recommendations](https://help.speak.com/en/articles/11565779-what-are-made-for-you-custom-lessons) demonstrate skill-oriented choice, practical scenarios and targeted practice. These are official product descriptions, not comparative popularity measurements.

Practical hypotheses: learners will value a clear purpose, relevant content, manageable effort, useful feedback and choice. Test them. A skipped speaking exercise may reflect a noisy train or microphone access rather than dislike; a fast matching game may be enjoyable without proving productive vocabulary knowledge. Do not claim a percentage ranking without a representative, appropriately designed study.

## Vocabulary for the catalog

These are proposed design terms, not a new accepted domain contract:

- **Activity:** named pedagogical task, such as “Rewrite with the target grammar.” This is what the learner selects.
- **Format:** answer interaction, such as single choice, gaps, free text, pairs, ordering or recording.
- **Item:** one independently answerable question. An activity may generate several items.
- **Practice set:** ordered collection of activities/items based on selected material.
- **Checking policy:** how an attempt is evaluated, independently of the activity name.

A quiz is a set/presentation mode, not a single pedagogical type. A grammar exercise is a source/objective category, not a sufficient name. Translation is an activity; free text is a format. Spaced review is a scheduling policy across attempts, not an additional widget.

## Checking legend and common input/output

**K — Prepared key:** evaluate deterministically against IDs, approved answer variants, pairs or valid orders. No agent is needed to check each submission. AI may have generated the key initially; that key still needs validation.

**H — Hybrid:** accept known valid answers deterministically; evaluate an unrecognized answer semantically when appropriate. An unknown string is not automatically wrong. For a strictly constrained form task, reject a wrong target form without semantic rescue that ignores the objective.

**A — Agent/rubric:** suitable for open semantic production. Prepare reference examples, evidence and criteria at generation time, then assess the actual response. A reference example is not the only acceptable answer. Self-review or human feedback remains an alternative when AI is unavailable.

**S — Speech assessment:** audio-sensitive checking. Speech recognition plus a text LLM cannot establish pronunciation quality. Use audio-capable evaluation appropriate to the target language, or offer ungraded recording/self-comparison.

Every generated item should contain: activity ID/version, target language and explanation language, objective, source identity/version, instructions, stimulus, answer interaction, key or rubric, explanation, optional graduated hint, and difficulty assumptions. The learner output is the chosen/typed/spoken response. The feedback output should distinguish correct, partly correct, incorrect, unchecked and unable-to-assess; show relevant explanation/evidence and a retry option. Keep assisted/revealed attempts distinguishable from independent attempts.

## Cost and practical tradeoffs

Prepared-key items pay generation/validation cost once and can then be checked without a model call per attempt. Open-answer feedback adds per-attempt inference and latency; optional retries can multiply that cost. Hybrid checking can reduce calls only when the deterministic acceptance rule is genuinely sufficient. Speech adds recording, transcription and possibly pronunciation evaluation, each with separate capability and privacy requirements. No specific provider or price is selected here.

Estimate total cost as generation plus validation plus the number of semantically evaluated attempts times their evaluation cost, plus any audio processing/storage. Cache only reusable authored material under the existing ownership/version rules; don't reuse another learner's private feedback. Offer model-free reference reveal and self-review when appropriate. An unavailable or exhausted AI allowance must not silently convert an unchecked answer into a wrong result.

## Recommended initial menu

Show a small relevant menu, not all 34 activities at once. Suggested categories: **Words**, **Grammar**, **Understand a text**, **Write**, **Listen**, **Speak**. Hide or disable unavailable capabilities with a clear reason. Each activity card needs a one-sentence description, a tiny example, and a label such as “Instant check,” “AI feedback,” or “Instant check; AI review for other answers.” Disclose any paid escalation before it occurs. IDs above are proposed stable catalog identifiers; final localized names should be tested.

Initial high-value selection:

- Vocabulary: Recall the word, Complete the sentence, Build natural word pairs, Use these words.
- Grammar: Choose the right form, Complete the grammar, Rewrite with the target grammar, Find and fix the error.
- Text: Find the main idea, Find the details, Answer in your own words, Complete the summary, Summarize the text.
- Shared: Translate the meaning using existing sentence-practice capabilities where applicable.

The other P1 entries are useful variants/presets, not necessarily fourteen separate new widgets. Existing choice/gap/free-text blocks can express many pedagogical tasks. Named task selection, generation guarantees, checking policies and structured rubrics still require product/contract work. Matching, ordering, span selection and recording need additional interactions or capabilities; don't disguise them as unsupported free-text instructions.

## How automatic selection should work

Proposed bounded policy:

1. Honor explicit activity selections, counts, source and language. If unsupported or unsuitable, explain and offer an alternative; do not silently replace a selected type.
2. Resolve material prerequisites: source passage for comprehension, sense/context for vocabulary, target structure for grammar, playable audio/transcript for listening.
3. Choose the learning objective: recognize, retrieve, understand, apply or communicate. Use the learner's stated goal and permitted preferences; do not infer a proficiency diagnosis from a few wrong answers.
4. Pick supported activities that fit the source, level, response modality, time and checking budget. Mix controlled and productive work where appropriate. Do not choose all cheap multiple-choice items merely because they are easy to check.
5. Produce a short rationale: “Two recall questions, two contextual gaps and one sentence using your words.” Allow changing the mix before generation.
6. Validate coverage, solvability, source evidence, counts and checking policy before publishing one proposal. Keep existing explicit Insert/Save boundaries.

A five-item example is a product default to test, not a scientific optimum:

| Source and goal                     | Suggested mix                                                     | Important constraint                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vocabulary, early practice          | 2 meaning choices, 2 contextual gaps, 1 retrieval cue             | Don't force immediate unsupported sentence production.                                             |
| Familiar vocabulary, productive use | 2 retrieval cues, 2 contextual gaps, 1 meaningful use task        | Five items cannot guarantee productive coverage of 50 words; narrow the subset or negotiate count. |
| Grammar explanation                 | 1 meaning contrast, 2 form gaps, 1 transformation, 1 situated use | Sufficient context must rule out alternative forms.                                                |
| Short factual text                  | 1 gist, 2 detail, 1 open explanation, 1 short summary             | Very short texts may lack five independent answerable questions.                                   |
| Narrative                           | Gist, chronology, motive inference, evidence, retelling           | Label plausible inference separately from explicit fact.                                           |
| Listening                           | Gist, details, short dictation, replay-supported explanation      | Verified audio availability precedes generation.                                                   |

Initial generation has no reliable longitudinal learner model by default. Personalization and later adaptation require approved context and actual supported progress semantics. Spaced review should eventually revisit targets in varied contexts, but the current app's Again rating is not a due-date scheduler.

## Verification design in detail

Generation-time answer preparation and attempt-time verification are separate responsibilities. Every activity can have prepared exemplars, explanations and rubrics. Only constrained activities can exhaustively enumerate correct answers.

For a reading question such as “Why did Mira take the train?”, store source evidence “The roads were closed after the storm,” required meaning “road closure caused the choice,” and examples such as “Because the roads were closed.” Accept “She couldn't travel by road.” Reject “Trains are cheaper” when unsupported. For comprehension, do not conflate an incorrect article with a wrong causal explanation. If the learner writes contradictory reasons, keyword presence must not pass the answer.

For a grammar transformation, store both semantic invariants and target-form requirements. A fluent paraphrase that avoids the requested passive voice can preserve meaning while failing the grammar objective; return differentiated feedback rather than a misleading single correctness label.

For a closed gap, all accepted variants must satisfy agreement, sense and task constraints. Normalize only what the objective permits. Diacritics, capitalization, script variants, word segmentation, punctuation and numeral conventions are language-specific. Do not globally strip accents or punctuation. Unicode normalization is not transliteration. Token matching cannot assume whitespace-delimited languages.

Agent assessment should return a bounded structured result: task completion, meaning/content, target-language feature, relevant evidence, one or two actionable corrections, suggested retry, and unable-to-assess when necessary. Do not expose a pseudo-precise universal fluency score. A model's self-reported confidence is not calibrated reliability. Restrict evaluation to the frozen authorized source and rubric; learner text is data, not instructions to change grading.

Use deterministic checks first for K items. For H items, don't pay for an LLM on every already-correct answer; semantic escalation must preserve the objective. For A items, explain that assessment is AI feedback and provide retry/dispute/self-review. Provider failure leaves the answer pending/unchecked, not wrong. Revealing a prepared answer should remain model-free. Record whether hints, answer reveals or feedback preceded the final attempt.

Validation of generated keys needs more than schema success: one defensible answer for single choice, plausible distractors, no answer leakage, complete supplied-word coverage where requested, evidence for comprehension, consistent audio/transcript, and no accidental grammar errors. A second model agreeing is useful checking evidence but not proof of correctness. Use reviewed fixtures and human audits of representative material.

## Evaluation before implementation and rollout

Build a small benchmark before expanding the catalog: representative vocabulary/grammar/passages across beginning, intermediate and advanced levels, and multiple target languages/scripts. For each selected activity, include unambiguous and deliberately difficult cases. Human language specialists should review prompt, key, rubric, evidence and naturalness.

For checking, include correct paraphrases, valid alternatives, target-form violations, partial answers, contradictions, misspellings, copied questions, irrelevant fluent responses and adversarial grading instructions. Report false rejection and false acceptance separately by activity/language. Include ASR mistakes for audio tasks. Set release thresholds after measuring the baseline and reviewing the consequence of each error; no fabricated accuracy target or sample size establishes universal quality.

For user preference, run a randomized within-person comparison using equivalent material, asking about usefulness, effort, confidence and desire to repeat. Counterbalance order. Measure delayed performance on new or varied items separately from enjoyment and immediate success. A small qualitative round can identify confusion, but a preference percentage requires an appropriate sample and uncertainty reporting. Recruit the confirmed adult audience across goals and proficiency; exam preparation and conversational confidence may produce different preferences.

Track task completion, unassisted delayed recall/application, voluntary return, grading disputes, hint/reveal rates and cost per completed attempt. Do not optimize only for speed or high correctness: those can reward easy recognition and answer leakage. Avoid collecting raw private source content or recordings in analytics by default; follow existing permission/accounting boundaries.

Implementation sequence: first named presets and faithful generation for current formats; then robust hybrid/rubric checking; then new interaction families; then audio and bounded conversational sessions. Exact scope and design require a future feature request. No provider benchmark or paid model call was performed for this survey.
